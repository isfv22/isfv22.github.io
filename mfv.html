Masters of Flow Visualization

The Masters of Flow Visualization (MFV) returns for its third edition at ISFV22 Madrid, following the first edition at ISFV20 and the second at ISFV21. The event brings young researchers together to learn from leading experts and to put their own visualization skills into practice, celebrating the idea that flow visualization is as much a craft as it is a measurement technique.

The event

How it works

The Masters of Flow Visualization will take place on Sunday, 11 July 2027 at the Campus Madrid - Puerta de Toledo, on the opening day of ISFV22.

The day is built around four masterclasses of 30 minutes, two in the morning and two in the afternoon, each given by an invited expert on one visualization technique. Each pair of masterclasses is followed by a hands-on session at the experimental stations of those techniques.

Participants work in teams of three to four, with up to six teams. At each station a team has 30 minutes to tune the experiment, acquire its images and process the data, combining their own experience with what they have just learned. The experiments are prepared and tested in advance, so that the time is spent on the visualization itself.

A panel of experts evaluates the teams on both technical execution and scientific creativity.

Participation

How to apply

Application deadline: 31 March 2027 (end of early-bird registration)

Participation is by application and places are limited. The Masters of Flow Visualization is open to registered ISFV22 participants, so applications close together with early-bird registration.

  1. Register for ISFV22

    Register for the conference first; early-bird registration is open until 31 March 2027. See Registration.

  2. Prepare your application

    • a letter of motivation describing your interest, experience and visualization work;
    • one image of a flow visualization you made;
    • a title and a short description of the image (150–200 words).

    Put the letter, the title and the description in a single PDF, and attach the image as a JPG or PNG file.

  3. Send one email to isfv22@uc3m.es

    Use this subject line, with your surname, and give in the email the name under which you registered for ISFV22:

    ISFV22 MFV APPLICATION - Surname

  4. Selection

    Selected applicants will be notified after the deadline. Participation in the Masters of Flow Visualization is free.

Who can apply

The event is open to young researchers — graduate students, postdocs and early-career researchers from academia or industry. An affinity with one or more flow visualization techniques is welcome. Participation is free, but limited to registered ISFV22 attendees.

Awards

The best-performing team across the selected flow visualization techniques will be recognized at the end of the event.

Science in motion

Masterclass · Masters of Flow Visualization

Lorène Champougny

CNRS Researcher, Laboratoire de Génie Chimique (LGC), Toulouse, France

Soap film iridescence — from colors to thickness maps

In the early 18th century, Sir Isaac Newton reported that thin films of water produce color fringes and realized that the color encodes the film thickness. Three centuries later, iridescence remains one of the most elegant tools to visualize flows in thin liquid films. This masterclass revisits the physical origin of soap film iridescence: interference between white light reflected from the two interfaces of a thin film selects a color that depends on the local thickness. We will derive the interference condition and show how it can be modeled and inverted to reconstruct the film thickness in time and space. We will discuss different experimental implementations of this technique — monochromatic fringes, RGB color matching, white-light reflectometry — and their limitations. Finally, we will illustrate how flows such as drainage and marginal regeneration become directly visible in the shifting colors of soap films.

About the lecturer

Lorène Champougny is a CNRS research scientist working at the Laboratoire de Génie Chimique (Chemical Engineering Center) in Toulouse, France, since 2025. She received her PhD from Paris-Saclay University in 2015 and was subsequently a postdoctoral fellow at ESPCI in Paris and at Universidad Carlos III de Madrid, Spain. Her research aims to understand the structure and evolution of multiphase liquid architectures, such as thin films, foams, and emulsions. Her work combines experiments and modeling to reveal how liquid flow and interfacial phenomena (capillary forces, interfacial rheology, transfers) couple in these systems. Her doctoral research was awarded a L'Oréal-UNESCO For Women in Science fellowship (2014), and she was granted a European Marie Curie postdoctoral fellowship in 2020.
