Flow visualization

Science in motion

Leonardo da Vinci Memorial Lecture

Fulvio Scarano

Professor of Aerodynamics, Faculty of Aerospace Engineering, Delft University of Technology, The Netherlands

Visualization of Flows and Surface Stresses: Where Do We Stand?

Flow visualization is accompanying fluid mechanics from its origins, with physical discoveries and the aesthetic beauty of flow generated patterns. The duality of bulk flow diagnostics and surface flow visualization still separates techniques like oil-flow, tufts, pressure and temperature sensitive paints from particle image velocimetry or smoke lines and wires. A unified approach that merges the flow diagnostics around the object and the processes at the fluid-solid interface is still missing. Where are we standing in this respect?

Citing JD Anderson “The only mechanisms Nature has for communicating a force to a body moving through a fluid are pressure and shear stress on the body surface”. Thus, accessing the surface flow properties unveils how the airflow exerts the forces causing motions and deformations of the object immersed in it.

The lecture discusses present efforts and a plausible future, based on the advancements in the domain of particle tracking, large-scale seeding and multi-directional volumetric systems. When combined with powerful data assimilation or computational techniques the global distribution of pressure and shear stress at the surface might be at reach. Can flow visualization be transformed into a holistic instrument that captures aerodynamic loads and forces alongside describing the flow patterns for phenomenological analysis?

About the speaker

Fulvio Scarano is Professor of Aerodynamics at Delft University of Technology, where he has worked since 2000 and has held a full professorship since 2008. He earned his PhD in Aerospace Engineering in 2000, following research at the University of Naples Federico II and the von Kármán Institute for Fluid Dynamics. His work on iterative image-deformation methods helped shape modern quantitative PIV; he is a co-pioneer of tomographic PIV, and his group extended volumetric measurements to industrial wind tunnels and full-scale applications with helium-filled soap bubbles. He has received ERC Starting, Proof-of-Concept and Advanced Grants, chaired ISFV20 in Delft (2023) and initiated the Masters of Flow Visualization. He is the recipient of the ISFV22 Leonardo da Vinci Award.

Carlomagno Award Lecture

Miguel A. Mendez

Associate Professor, Université libre de Bruxelles and von Karman Institute for Fluid Dynamics, Belgium

Beyond the Grid: Meshless Analysis and Modeling of Lagrangian Flow Data

Lagrangian approaches are gaining increasing importance in fluid mechanics, from particle tracking velocimetry (PTV) in experimental fluid dynamics to particle-based numerical methods such as Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics (SPH). These techniques produce data scattered in space and evolving with the flow, challenging conventional approaches to flow analysis and visualization. While mapping these data onto Eulerian grids enables the use of established post-processing tools, working directly with their native representation offers promising opportunities.

This lecture explores recent developments towards meshless analysis and modeling of Lagrangian flow data. We first introduce physics-constrained Radial Basis Function (RBF) regression as a framework for constructing continuous, differentiable flow representations directly from scattered measurements. We then illustrate how this framework enables binless turbulence statistics, adaptive and anisotropic reconstructions, and meshless modal decompositions through RBF-induced inner products.

Finally, we explore the extension of these ideas towards Lagrangian POD–Galerkin reduced-order modeling, illustrated through an application to SPH simulations of sloshing flows. Together, these advances point towards a broader class of meshless methods connecting flow visualization, statistical analysis, and reduced-order modeling, while preserving the flexibility of Lagrangian representations.

About the speaker

Miguel A. Mendez is Associate Professor at the Université libre de Bruxelles and the von Karman Institute for Fluid Dynamics (VKI), and holds the honorary title of Profesor Honorífico at Universidad Carlos III de Madrid. He earned his PhD in Engineering at VKI and ULB in 2018. His work bridges experimental fluid mechanics, quantitative flow visualization, data-driven methods and flow control. Through physics-constrained radial-basis-function regression, his research reconstructs continuous flow fields directly from irregular image-velocimetry and particle-tracking data, supported by the open-source software SPICY. He developed the multiscale proper orthogonal decomposition (mPOD), available with other modal-analysis methods in the open-source framework MODULO. His ERC Starting Grant on Reinforcement Twinning investigates the joint learning of a digital twin and a control policy. He received a Measurement Science and Technology Outstanding Paper Award (2020) and was named an Emerging Leader by the journal (2022); his doctoral research was recognized with the Solvay PhD Thesis Award and the AFVL prize for laser diagnostics. He directs the annual Hands-on Machine Learning for Fluid Dynamics course. He is the recipient of the ISFV22 Carlomagno Award.

Plenary lecture

Sergey B. Leonov

Professor, Department of Aerospace and Mechanical Engineering, University of Notre Dame, USA

Application of Mie Scattering for Precision Visualization of High-Speed Flow Features

Laser-scattering visualization using artificially seeded particles is a well-established, non-intrusive approach for studying complex supersonic flows. However, conventional methods, such as PIV, can be challenging in compressible flows because of the difficulty of achieving uniform seeding with externally introduced solid or liquid particles. Mie scattering based on naturally condensed particles formed during gas expansion in a supersonic nozzle (M = 2–6) provides an attractive and cost-effective alternative. It enables both qualitative visualization of low-contrast flow structures and quantitative flow measurements through the strong dependence of scattered-light intensity on gas temperature and pressure. Two applications will be presented: visualization of sidewall vortices in a rectangular M = 4 wind-tunnel test section and quantitative characterization of M = 6 swirling flow in a free-jet configuration. CO2 nucleation dynamics and particle-size estimates will also be discussed.

These examples demonstrate the capability of Mie scattering to address complex flow-diagnostic challenges using a relatively simple and affordable approach. The method’s limitations, recent advances, and future prospects will also be discussed.

About the speaker

Sergey B. Leonov is a Professor in the Department of Aerospace and Mechanical Engineering at the University of Notre Dame, USA, where he has worked since 2014. He received his PhD from the Baltic State University in 1990 and a senior Doctor of Science degree from the Russian Academy of Sciences in 2006. He was Head of Department at the Joint Institute for High Temperatures of the Russian Academy of Sciences and, concurrently, a Professor at Moscow Open University, and in 2013–2014 a Visiting Professor at The Ohio State University. His research interests and contributions are in plasma aerodynamics, flow physics, supersonic combustion, active flow control, weakly ionized plasmas, and experimental methods and instrumentation. He has over 400 publications, including over 100 peer-reviewed journal papers, book chapters and patents. He is an Associate Fellow of AIAA, an IEEE Senior Member and an Honorary Member of FLUCOME. He coordinates the Plasma Aerodynamics Discussion Group, serves on the AIAA Plasmadynamics and Lasers and the EUCASS Flow Physics technical committees, and is Section Editor-in-Chief of the journal Aerospace and a Section Editor of Technical Physics Letters. He is a recipient of the Yasuki Nakayama Medal “In recognition of professional standing and outstanding contributions in the field of flow management, measurement, and visualization.”

Plenary lecture

Yang Xu

Professor, School of Aeronautic Science and Engineering, Beihang University, Beijing, China

Phosphorescence-Based Simultaneous Measurements of Velocity and Temperature

Applications to impinging jets and physics-informed flow-field reconstruction

Many thermal-fluid problems require measurements of velocity and temperature at the same time: neither field alone adequately describes the coupling between momentum and heat transport. Particle image and particle tracking velocimetry provide velocity information, whereas fluorescence, liquid-crystal thermometry and thermographic phosphors can reveal temperature. Bringing these measurements together remains an important challenge for quantitative flow visualization.

This lecture will describe a phosphorescence-based method for simultaneous velocity–temperature measurement using functional tracer particles. Particle trajectories provide velocity, while phosphorescence-lifetime decay provides temperature. Applications to impinging synthetic jets and cold jets illustrate the ability of the technique to resolve coupled thermal and flow structures.

The second part addresses the complementary role of physics-informed reconstruction. Experimentally measured velocity fields are used as inputs to physics-informed neural networks to infer pressure and temperature distributions. The approach is discussed in connection with vortex rings impinging on porous walls, where reconstructed pressure fields help characterize forces and drag. Together, these examples connect optical measurement, data-driven reconstruction and the interpretation of complex vortex–wall interactions.

About the speaker

Yang Xu is a Professor in the School of Aeronautic Science and Engineering at Beihang University, Beijing, China. He received his PhD in Fluid Mechanics from Beihang University in 2017, following a BS in Engineering Mechanics (2011); his doctoral training included research at George Washington University (2015–2016), and he visited Pusan National University in 2018. Appointed full professor in 2024, he investigates quantitative optical diagnostics and vortex dynamics, combining experiments with physics-based modelling and data-driven field reconstruction. His contributions include a phosphorescence-lifetime technique that uses functional tracer particles both for particle-tracking velocimetry and for thermometry, and time-resolved PIV/LIF studies of vortex rings passing through porous walls, featured on two Journal of Fluid Mechanics covers (2018 and 2026). His recognitions include the National Postdoctoral Program for Innovative Talents (2017), Beijing’s Young Top Talents programme (2021), an Excellent Youth Project of the National Natural Science Foundation of China (2023) and a Chinese Society of Aeronautics and Astronautics natural-science prize (2024). He helped organize FLUCOME 2023 and IUTAM-TNTI 2024 and serves on the ISFV International Scientific Committee.
